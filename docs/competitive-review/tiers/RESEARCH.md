## How Inside Story can make more of itself pay caliber

1. **Charge for what the app works out, not for what the person writes down.** Every competitor that succeeds draws the same line: Bearable, Daylio, mySymptoms and Cronometer give logging away and charge for understanding it. Inside Story’s paid core is the reading nobody else does: every food, meal and dose read against 19 conditions, Pattern Finder, Therapy Response, the food or supplement split and the reports. Everything a person records stays Free and is never locked. Rests on: [Logging how you feel, symptoms and reactions](#fam-symptoms), [Finding what tends to come before a flare](#fam-pattern), [Reading a food against your conditions](#fam-condition-food), [Nutrients from food and from supplements](#fam-nutrients).
2. **Make commodity trackers earn their place by what they feed.** Weight, sleep, steps, water and blood pressure cannot sell alone, since phones give them away. They become worth paying for when they sit beside something: sleep beside flares in Pattern Finder, water read for kidney disease or gout, blood pressure beside sodium. Keep each tracker Free and make the "beside" view paid. Rests on: [Weight, sleep, steps, blood pressure and workouts](#fam-body), [Hydration](#fam-hydration).
3. **Use unlimited reminders as the Free tier’s advertisement.** Medisafe cut its free tier to two medicines in January 2026 and users revolted; most people with a chronic condition take four or five. Unlimited reminders on Free, said plainly in the store listing, takes that market. The timing around them (meals and doses interleaved, tapering, dose history) is the paid step. Rests on: [Medication reminders and dose records](#fam-med-reminders).
4. **Sell privacy as the reason the app costs money.** Free health apps have been paid for with data: GoodRx and Flo were fined, Mozilla flagged 29 of 32 mental health apps, and Mint closed when credit referrals stopped covering it. People say they would pay about $12 more for strong data safety. Inside Story has no server, so the price is the whole business model. Say so on the paywall, while the lock itself stays Free. Rests on: [Privacy, the lock, the vault and backup](#fam-privacy), [Money, medical costs and insurance](#fam-money), [Menstrual cycle](#fam-cycle).
5. **Give the second audience a paid way in that needs no condition.** People with ADHD already pay $79.99 a year for Tiimo, $27 to $40 for Routinery and $99.99 for Structured. Inside Story’s routines, Did I Do It, Keeping Up, Where did I put it, the one next thing and Ready for tomorrow match that and add the rest of life. Today the paid tier is priced around condition scoring. Worth deciding: does someone who came for daily living pay the same Individual price, or is there a lower daily-living price that upgrades into health? Rests on: [Daily living: the next thing, routines, to-dos, capture](#fam-daily), [What is on hand and where it is](#fam-inventory).
6. **Treat the garden as paid depth that people already pay for.** Garden planners charge $25 to $168 a year. The Inside Story garden is the only one tied to the kitchen and the body, and "What to grow for my conditions" (I16) would be unique. Plots and the harvest log Free; sowing calendar, tasks, yield, sensors, crop guides and the garden report paid. The hive log is a candidate for a separate add-on, since HiveTracks charges by hive. Rests on: [Garden planning and harvest](#fam-garden), [Growing conditions, sensors and grow setup](#fam-sensors), [Living soil, compost and crop guides](#fam-soil), [Bees and the hive log](#fam-bees).
7. **Show the bundle in dollars.** Bought separately, the closest single apps cost: Cronometer Gold $59.99, Bearable $34.99, Medisafe $39.99, Eat This Much from $47.99, Tiimo $79.99, YNAB $109, Seedtime from $84 and Day One $49.99, about $506 a year, before the 19-condition scoring that nobody sells at all. Against that, $89.99 a year is easy to explain, and the paywall should explain it.
8. **Fix the annual price.** Health and fitness leans to annual plans and keeps people longer on them. $89.99 a year is only 25% off twelve months at $9.99, where about 50% is the norm. An annual price of $59.99 to $69.99 would match Cronometer Gold and Fig while keeping the monthly price. A lifetime price and a hardship price (Z15) are still the owner’s call.
9. **Let Free taste the paid core, a little.** Freemium health apps convert about 4% of users; a hard paywall converts 12% of far fewer. Showing one paid result on Free (one For You reading a day, one generated day of meals, the top Pattern Finder result) lets someone see what they would get, the way Eat This Much gives one free day. A trial of everything that drops back to Free afterwards is the other common approach; trial-to-paid in health averages about 40%. Rests on: [Reading a food against your conditions](#fam-condition-food), [Meal planning](#fam-meal-plan), [Finding what tends to come before a flare](#fam-pattern).
10. **Price the companions on the work they do for the payer.** Sharing that brings in new users is free in the market (Clue Connect, AnyList lists); plans that do work for the payer are paid (Tiimo family, Medfriend). So: viewer companions Free, since each viewer meets the app; Partner at about 1.5x; Caregiver per person, the strongest of all because caregivers carry the most and nothing else holds records for them. Guardian, bundled free with a paid plan, is a reason to choose that plan. Rests on: [Companion tiers: partner, household, guardian, caregiver](#fam-companions).
11. **Start the planned work at pay caliber.** Several planned functions already have a paying market: a life’s Inside Story and If something happens to me (Everplans $99.99 a year), family health history (Ancestry $39.99 a month, MyHeritage about $299 a year), making your own Learn decks (Quizlet $35.99), the diary (Day One $49.99), the hive log (HiveTracks about $69.99). Pregnancy with a condition in view and insurance appeals have no competitor at all. Rests on: [If something happens to me, and a life’s Inside Story](#fam-legacy), [Family health history and the family tree](#fam-family-tree), [Learn](#fam-learn), [The Diary](#fam-diary), [Bees and the hive log](#fam-bees), [Pregnancy and postpartum](#fam-womens), [Money, medical costs and insurance](#fam-money).
12. **What never moves to paid.** Safety (P28): the emergency card, interaction and timing warnings, allergy cautions, the safety plan. Accessibility: Low Stimulation, hand switching, text size and anything past it (P9). Getting records out in open formats (P6), because the record belongs to the person and resume builders show what charging for a download looks like. And ending a shared relationship (P32), which must never depend on who pays. Rests on: [Safety: interactions, allergies and the emergency card](#fam-med-safety), [Looks, accessibility and the way around](#fam-looks), [A mental health safety plan](#fam-mental).

## Why things are free or paid: the kinds

- **Paid for with data or ads.** The person is the product. Free health and finance apps have been paid for by advertising, referral fees and data sharing, and regulators have fined some of them for it.
- **Comes with a phone or a device.** Apple, Google and hardware makers give the app away because it sells the phone, the ring or the sensor.
- **A free tier that sells the paid one.** Logging is given away so that the analysis, history or export can be charged for. Health apps convert about 4% of free users this way.
- **Every app has it.** Simple to build and offered by dozens of apps, so nobody can charge for it alone.
- **Paid for by a nonprofit, a church or a government.** Donors, grants or public money cover it, so it is free to everyone and stays that way.
- **Sold once.** A one-time purchase rather than a subscription, which sets what people expect to pay.
- **Nobody gives it away.** Every app reviewed charges for it.
- **Nobody offers it.** No app reviewed does this, so there is no price to compare against.

### Medication reminders and dose records

Kinds: Paid for with data or ads, A free tier that sells the paid one, Every app has it.

**Why it is free or paid elsewhere.** Reminders are the most common health app feature there is: 92% of adherence apps do nothing else. MyTherapy is free because pharmaceutical companies pay for it. When Medisafe capped its free tier at two medicines, users revolted, because most people with a chronic condition take four or five.

| App | Price | Free | Paid | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medisafe | Free for 2 meds; $39.99 a year | Two medications | More medications, Medfriend, reports | <https://pillo.care/blog/medisafe-not-free-what-to-do> schedules.md §2.1 |

| CareClinic | Free; Premium about $69.99 a year | Reminders | Reports, care team | <https://www.capterra.com/p/181945/CareClinic/> schedules.md §2.3 |

- Of 421 free adherence apps, 13.8% involved health professionals and 0.95% had any evidence base. 92% were reminders only. [JMIR mHealth 2018: medication adherence apps](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/3/e62/PDF)
- Medisafe cut its free tier to two medications in January 2026 (Premium $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year). People with a chronic condition take four to five on average. Users objected loudly. [Pillo: Medisafe is no longer free](https://pillo.care/blog/medisafe-not-free-what-to-do)

**How it reaches pay caliber.** Unlimited reminders on Free, said out loud as the opposite of Medisafe. The timing intelligence around them (Today’s Meals, taper plans, dose history over time) is paid.

On the board: Today's Meals: meals and doses side by side with timing checks (Schedules); Dose reminders and timeline (Schedules); Pills on hand and refill reminders (Schedules); Tapering doses (Schedules); Injection site rotation (Schedules); Travel: keep home time or shift to local (Schedules); Doses Over Time (Trends); My Meds (the list itself) (Life); Reminders you can answer from the notification (Across the app).

## General evidence

- [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025 (via Athletech News)](https://athletechnews.com/fitness-apps-monetizable-winner-take-all-or-most/). Health and fitness has the highest revenue per install of any category, a $0.63 median 60 days after install. Trial-to-paid median 39.9%, top tenth 68.3%. The category leans to annual plans.
- [RevenueCat report, ten learnings (SaaStr)](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth). iPhone users convert about twice as well as Android users. Annual plans keep people longer than monthly ones.
- [iOS free-to-paid conversion benchmarks 2026](https://appsops.store/blog/ios-free-to-paid-conversion-benchmarks-2026). Freemium median 2.18% across categories, 4.2% for health and fitness, 6 to 8% for the best apps.
- [Adapty: freemium to premium conversion](https://adapty.io/blog/freemium-to-premium-conversion-techniques). A hard paywall converts at a 12.11% median, several times a freemium tier, but reaches far fewer people.
- [Pillo: Medisafe is no longer free](https://pillo.care/blog/medisafe-not-free-what-to-do). Medisafe cut its free tier to two medications in January 2026 (Premium $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year). People with a chronic condition take four to five on average. Users objected loudly.
- [MobiHealthNews: GoodRx and the FTC](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/goodrx-shared-health-data-google-and-facebook-ftc-says). GoodRx paid $1.5 million for sharing health data with Google and Facebook, the first action under the Health Breach Notification Rule. Flo Health settled with the FTC in 2021 over sharing data with the same companies.
- [Mozilla Privacy Not Included: mental health apps](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/ca/privacynotincluded/articles/getting-private-mental-health-and-prayer-apps-rush-to-revise-privacy-policies/). Of 32 mental health and prayer apps, 29 earned a privacy warning for sharing data, weak passwords and targeted ads.
- [JMIR mHealth 2018: medication adherence apps](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/3/e62/PDF). Of 421 free adherence apps, 13.8% involved health professionals and 0.95% had any evidence base. 92% were reminders only.
- [MobiHealthNews: patients want their doctors to prescribe apps](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/most-patients-want-their-doctors-prescribe-apps). 90% of 2,000 people with a chronic condition would use an app their doctor prescribed.
- [MobiHealthNews: what people would pay more for in a health app](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/asia/koreans-would-pay-more-feature-mobile-health-apps). People would pay about $12 more for strong data safety and about $5 more for accreditation.
- [Smartpatient (MyTherapy): how it is paid for](https://www.smartpatient.eu/pharma-services/patient-support). MyTherapy is free because pharmaceutical companies pay for adherence programs built on it and receive aggregated adherence data.
- [NerdWallet: Mint is closing](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/mint-app-closing-what-it-means-how-to-pick-a-new-budget-service). Mint was free for 15 years, paid for by credit card and loan referrals, and shut in March 2024 when that stopped covering its costs.
- [Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/). A volunteer nonprofit database of packaged foods, funded by donations and free for any app to use. Yuka and most barcode apps build on it.
- [Day One pricing guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/premium-subscription/day-one-pricing-features-guide/). Day One Silver (formerly Premium) $49.99 a year, Gold $74.99 a year.
- [Church of Jesus Christ: free family history access](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/church/news/church-members-gain-free-access-to-178-billion-family-history-records?lang=eng). FamilySearch is free to everyone, run by the church as a nonprofit.
- [The Pricer: what Ancestry costs](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-ancestry-cost/). Ancestry World Explorer $39.99 a month or $149 for six months. MyHeritage Complete about $299 a year.
- [Mindomax: Quizlet or Anki](https://www.mindomax.com/quizlet-vs-anki). Anki is free on computers, Android and the web (volunteer project), AnkiMobile is $24.99 once. Quizlet Plus $35.99 a year.
- [Guideflow: beekeeping software compared](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/beekeeping-software). HiveTracks $5 to $20 a month by hive count (about $69.99 a year in the app). Apiary Book free with paid plans from about EUR 3.33 a month.
- [Everplans: what it costs](https://help.everplans.com/hc/en-us/articles/215665778-How-much-does-Everplans-cost). Everplans Premium $99.99 a year; the free tier holds three items.
- [Apple: Legacy Contact](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/102678). Apple gives every account a free legacy contact who can reach its data after death.
- [CaringBridge](https://CaringBridge.org). A nonprofit, free, no ads and no data sales, over 90% funded by people who have used it.
- [iNaturalist: is it free?](https://help.inaturalist.org/en/support/solutions/articles/151000189329). Free with no paywall, a nonprofit funded by grants (Moore Foundation) and donations, with photo hosting donated by Amazon.
- [11pets on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/11pets-pet-care/id1232470530). Free with in-app purchases from $1.99 to $17.99.
- [Unstar: resume builder apps ranked 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/resume-io-zety-canva-indeed-novoresume-resume-builder-apps-ranked-2026). Most resume builders are free to write in and charge to download, often a $2.95 trial that renews at $23.95 every four weeks (about $311 a year). The FTC hears about 70 complaints a day on subscription traps.
