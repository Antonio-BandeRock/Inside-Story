## What this app is

**Inside Story** was chosen after ruling out Aurelia, Chrysalis, Monarch, Second Nature, Food Sense, FoodFlex, and Backstory, each of which had a real collision. It is a genuine English idiom, not an invented word.

**Never describe this as a Hashimoto's app, or even only an autoimmune-conditions app.** The creator's own framing: "everything has an inside story… This app started to help with Hashimoto's, then decided to help with 18 other conditions, then has grown from there to include basically everything in life revolving around a person." Describe it as an everything-about-a-person's-life app that started with, and runs deepest on, food and autoimmune health. Garden, budget, family, soil microbiome, and the body are all in scope.

**Scope, stated directly:** "this is no longer just focused on Hashimoto's, it is focused on all 19 both individually and if a person has multiple of them combined." Every one of the 18 non-Hashimoto's conditions is a standing target for the same depth, not a lesser tier. Combined-condition (polyautoimmunity) management is its own named scope item, not yet built as a first-class destination.

**Who it is for, past the 19 conditions.** Direct statement, 2026-09-16: "I would like for this app to not just be viewed as being for the 19 conditions. The Autism and ADHD additions don’t much have anything to do with food at all, but the app itself is the thing that will help people who do have those neurodivergent traits to cope with daily living and all of the details that do need to be kept track of and reminding them of and making sure they have a way to keep all of those things from being on their minds. That in itself, I think will be a boon for them."

So there is a second audience with as strong a claim on this app as the health one: someone who needs an outside place to hold the details of daily living, so those details stop taking up room in their head. ADHD and autism are the named cases, and the need is wider than that (anyone recovering, grieving, caregiving, or simply carrying too much). For that person the point of the app is not the food scoring at all. It is Life, Schedules, reminders, and capture. The health depth is there when they want it, not the price of entry.

Two things follow, and they change what gets built:

1. **A feature that serves only the 19 conditions is a narrower feature than one that also serves this.** When both are within reach for the same effort, build both.
2. **The way in must not require a condition.** Onboarding, the Free tier, and the store description all still lead with health conditions. Someone who came for help running their day should recognize the app as theirs on the first screen. Not built, and a named gap.

**Never frame this as diet or nutrition treating ADHD or autism.** Nutrition supports them; it does not treat them, which is the line the Digest’s own ADHD entries already take. The daily-living support stands on its own and needs no food claim attached to it.

### The core purpose (read before any scope trade-off)

Help someone with an autoimmune condition relearn how and what to eat, and understand, personally and for their own body, how food affects them. Meal building, shopping lists, and scheduling are scaffolding that make daily use sustainable enough to generate that understanding. They are not the point. **When in doubt about priority, favor whatever helps someone discover and act on their own personal food-effect patterns over convenience features.**

Gut and microbiome healing is an explicit goal, not a side effect. The app is meant to actively help repair gut health, not just avoid trigger foods.

**Food first, and a supplement only for what food cannot supply.** Direct statement, 2026-09-22: "the goal is to modify how a person eats so with their food they are receiving an optimal amount of every one of them and not have to take any supplements, but to also make sure that you do take the ones you cannot receive from your food because of whatever reason, such as being vegan, or alergic, etc." A target reached by food and the same target reached by a capsule are two different outcomes, and everywhere the app shows a nutrient amount it has to say which one happened. Two halves, and neither is advice to stop taking anything: show which supplements a person's eating has already made unnecessary, and show which ones their diet cannot cover, so those keep being taken. Built: Insights > Nutrients draws each row's food and supplement shares as one bar and says the division in words when the row is opened (1.0.49.5, `nutrientSourceSplit` in `lib/nutrientAnalysis.ts`), Home's Today's Fuel Gauges draw the supplement stretch of each ring in amber with a key line under the row (1.0.50.2, `supplementPercent` in `components/ProgressRing.tsx`), and My Meds says, at the moment somebody adds a supplement, how much of that target food is covering already. Trends > Nutrients draws the same division over the whole charted range (1.0.50.5): the lower line is food by itself, the amber space up to the top line is what a supplement added, tapping a day reads "118% (72% from food)", and under the chart sit the two averages and the count of days food by itself reached the target. The supplement half is worked out per day from each supplement's start and end dates (`getSupplementNutrientTotalsByDate`, `lib/supplementWindow.ts`), which also corrected the single total line: it used to stretch today's regimen across the whole range, so a supplement started yesterday read as having been there in June. Nothing in this app records a dose being swallowed, so where a person gave no dates the supplement is counted across the range as before and the caption says so.

**When a supplement is taken belongs to scheduling, and it matters.** Same statement: supplements carry rules about with or without food, with water or with fat, not with certain foods, and not with other vitamins, minerals, acids and hormones they compete with, synergistic and antagonistic both. Those rules live in the interaction-rules engine and the dose timeline in Schedules > Meds. Schedules > Today's Meals is the day itself, meals and doses interleaved by the clock, and an opened dose reads the meals around it (1.0.49.6; `lib/doseMealTiming.ts` checks a dose against the nearest qualifying MEAL, which `lib/interactionRules.ts` never did, and `getDayMealAndDoseTimeline` in `lib/db.ts` assembles the day). Two things there stay true whatever gets built on top of them: `MEAL_AMOUNT_THAT_MATTERS` is a set of working thresholds rather than doses from a trial, which is why every sentence it generates names the amount itself rather than asking to be trusted, and a meal with nothing resolvable to the food database reads as could not be checked, never as carrying nothing. Nothing on that lens writes; times, taken, skipped and removed stay on Meds, so one place holds that record. The build account is in `docs/CLAUDE-ARCHIVE-2026-09-14.md`.

The user's own reframing: people are overwhelmed and confused about food and can't put the pieces together themselves. The app's job is to remove that cognitive burden entirely, doing the correlation and discovery work *for* the person so daily eating becomes following clear, pre-solved rules. The complexity lives inside the app; what the person experiences is ease. **This makes discovery and trend-finding something the app does on the user's behalf, not a task handed to them.**

### International scope

Not US-centric. Three distinct components:

1. **Cited international and regional epidemiological data per condition:** how prevalence and severity vary by country or region, for all 19 conditions, with cited corroborating explanation (genetics, diet, environment, latitude, screening practices). One "Global Perspective" entry per condition, in progress.
2. **i18n architecture, decided but not built.** Languages: all common English variants (US, UK, AU), Spanish (Latin American and Spain), German, French, Finnish, Norwegian, Swedish, Japanese. **Chinese and Italian were removed 2026-08-10 by direct instruction.** No i18n library is wired in: no `expo-localization`, no `react-i18next`, no string extraction has been done. This is substantial engineering work, deliberately not half-started.
3. **Country-specific reference data.** The 22,022-food database already draws on 7 national sources (US, Canada, UK, Germany, Australia, France, Japan). Real gaps: no Spanish-speaking country's data, and no Nordic source yet.

## Standing rules

These are behavior-changing and apply to every session:

- **The Rule Engine, a standing name for a real, recurring gap.** Direct request, 2026-08-26, after the oxalate/calcium-pairing work below: "we need a word for this part of the app so we both know what we are talking about... it has to always apply to all of the ways the information needs to be wired up, so nothing ever gets left out." **The Rule Engine is every mechanism in this app that encodes one real, evidence-based nutrition or health fact as a reusable "if X then Y" rule about a food, ingredient, nutrient, or combination**, as opposed to the Digest's own long-form narrative writing. It is not one file or one table; it is a set of real, already-built pieces that all have to be checked whenever a genuinely new fact enters the app:
  1. **Reference-database scoring** (`sub_criteria`/`food_scores`, `assets/data/foods_reference.db`) -- does this fact need its own scored sub-criterion on individual foods?
  2. **Condition relevance** (`sub_criterion_condition_relevance`) -- which of the 19 tracked conditions does this scored property actually matter for? (See the 2026-08-24 Migraine relevance-mapping precedent, `scripts/add_migraine_condition_relevance.js`, for the safe, established way to extend this.)
  3. **Recipe condition-cautions** (`scripts/compute_recipe_condition_data.js`, feeding `lib/digest/recipes.ts`) -- does a flagged recipe need a real caution surfaced under "Meals You Can Eat," and is the SEVERITY genuinely calibrated (see the 2026-08-26 Oxalate Level/Oxalate Load Rank fix below for what happens when a raw measurement gets used as if it were the real verdict)?
  4. **The meal generator's own rule sets** (`lib/dailyMealPlan.ts`: `NUTRIENT_SYNERGY_RULES`, `NUTRIENT_ANTAGONISM_RULES`, `FREQUENCY_RULES`, and the pairing-requirement mechanism below) -- should this fact actively shape what gets recommended or flagged, not just described?
  5. **The interaction-rules engine** (`interaction_rules` table, `lib/interactionRules.ts`) -- is this really a timing/combination caution between a food, supplement, and/or prescription, distinct from a per-food score?
  6. **Digest condition-specific content** -- does a tracked condition this fact is genuinely relevant to deserve its own cited research entry explaining why?
  7. **Digest Basic Health content** -- is this a general-population-relevant fact worth its own entry regardless of which conditions someone tracks?
  8. **Healing-stage advisories** (`lib/*StageAdvisory.ts`) -- does this matter differently at different stages of a staged condition's own healing journey?

  Not every new fact needs all eight; a purely narrative finding (a documentary fact-check) may only need 6-7, and a pure food property may only need 1-3. The discipline is running through the list deliberately every time, not defaulting to "just write the Digest entry" and calling it done. Update this list itself if a genuinely new kind of wiring point gets built.

- **One distance between stacked bands, everywhere: `HOME_BAND_GAP` in `components/HomeSectionBand.tsx`.** Direct instruction, 2026-09-12: "Set that distance as the standard for distance between them on every part of the app." Every scrolling screen in the app takes the band look as of 2026-09-19: surfaces run edge to edge with a left accent in the tab's colour, `makeTabBandStyles(tabColor)` in `components/TabBand.tsx` hands a tab its card and heading bands, and any screen reads `HOME_BAND_GAP` for its column gap and for any band's gap to what follows it, never a retyped 10 or 14. The same applies to a band's inner content inset, `HOME_BAND_CONTENT_PADDING`, which is also the side margin of any button standing between bands. Scroll content carries no side padding and no bottom margin: the bottom padding comes inline from `useFloatingButtonScrollPadding`, a full window of run-out, so a background photo the person chose shows through past the last band. Bare lead, footnote and empty-state text is wrapped in a band rather than sitting on the photo. `homeBandStyle` carries View-only props, so it never goes on a `Text` directly.
- **The Digest owns full content; the tool areas own data.** Direct instruction, 2026-08-18: "Think of the Digest as being where we pass along full read knowledge and the rest of the areas are for collecting data, manipulating it, and reporting it in meaningful ways." The rule outlives the tab: since 2026-09-19 the reading lenses (Conditions, Health Literacy, Earth Matters on Life; Horticulture on Garden) are where full content lives. Tool-area screens use compact rows (one line of primary text, one muted caption); full descriptions open on tap via `showInfoAlert()`, never sitting permanently on screen. Data-entry forms are the exception and stay as `formCard`s.
- **Never frame Hashimoto's as more central than the other 18 tracked conditions** (memory key `feedback_no_hashimotos_primacy_framing`).
- **Home cooking over commercial and branded products.** Don't expand branded or commercial reference entries. The intended path for a store-bought item is the barcode scan, not a generic "commercial" or "préemballée" entry.
- **Writing style in app content, and in this document:** avoid the filler words "real," "genuine," and "genuinely." **No dashes used in place of ordinary punctuation:** no em dashes, no en dashes, and no " -- " double-hyphen pattern. Rewrite with a colon, a comma, parentheses, or a second sentence instead. Hyphens in ordinary compound words (self-declared, on-device, read-write) are fine. **No redundant "own"** in constructions like "this category's own research," "the trial's own authors," "glyphosate's own cancer classification," the possessive already establishes ownership, "own" adds nothing and isn't how people actually talk (2026-08-24 direct correction, "This is not how people talk... The word 'own' has no place there at all"). A rare, genuinely legitimate use survives: the "in its own right"/"on its own terms"/"on its own merits" idiom family, the standalone "on X's own" (independently) idiom, and the real self-vs-other medical framing in "attacks the body's own tissue." Everything else gets the "own" removed.

  **This rule is checkable now, so stop checking it by memory.** `node scripts/audit_ai_isms.js [paths]` walks a character-level scanner over `lib`, `app`, `components`, `constants` and `hooks`, separating string literals, JSX text runs, SQL template literals and code comments, and reports hits in 13 categories at two tiers: `block` (fix it) and `look` (a person decides). The categories are the filler words, redundant "own", dashes standing in for punctuation, commentary about the writing instead of the writing, announcing its own honesty, the "not X, it is Y" reversal and its cleft form, the "X is the Y" pronouncement, discourse markers nobody speaks, words nobody says out loud, the summing-up flourish, headline shapes, the hedge pivot, and the rule of three. Run it on anything you wrote before calling content work done. It reported 4,093 hits across 142 files when written, on 2026-09-18, and the whole-app sweep that followed is what brought that down.

  **`.claude/agents/ai-ism-editor.md` is the agent that does the removing.** Direct instruction, 2026-09-18: it runs on newly written or newly changed content only, never on a schedule and never on content nobody asked about, and its start and completion are announced every time. It reports a per-category tally with the before and after text of every change, because the tally is as much the deliverable as the edits. Claude Code reads `.claude/agents/` once, when a session starts, so an agent written during a session is missing from that session. That is what `Agent type 'ai-ism-editor' not found` meant through 1.0.40.1 to 1.0.40.4, each of which was swept by hand plus `scripts/audit_ai_isms.js` instead. The definition is valid and needs no editing; the fix is to restart Claude Code, and never to substitute a different agent, since the standing rule everywhere else is no subagents on this project.

  **Four standing consequences of the 2026-09-18 sweeps, each of which a later session will be tempted to undo:**
  1. **A Digest title says what the entry is about.** Direct instruction: "The title of something shouldn't make someone still wonder what they are about to read." The shape that was removed is a subject, a colon, and a phrase that promises rather than states ("Water: The One Nutrient With No Official Government Recommendation at All"). 116 titles were rewritten to name the subject and the finding. The colon-subtitle convention was explicitly rejected as house style, so do not reintroduce it, and do not write a new entry with one.
  2. **The `stageNote` field is gone from `DigestEntry` and from the renderer.** All 205 of them were deleted by direct instruction. They were small grey italic notes explaining why an entry had been included, which is curation rationale and belongs in a code comment. Do not add the field back and do not put the same content in `teaser` or `summary` instead.
  3. **The "already covered" cross-reference refrain is out of customer-facing text.** A bare self-reference ("already covered in this category") was deleted outright, since it points at nothing a reader can go to. A pointer naming a place a reader can actually reach was kept, minus the word "already", which presumes the reader has been through it. 27 instances survive entirely in code comments, which is where curation rationale belongs.
  4. **`OWNER_CHOICE` in `scripts/audit_ai_isms.js` holds two deliberate exemptions**, "Industry, Greenwashing & Honest Limits" and "Building Real Soil". Both were asked for back by name after a sweep had shortened them to quiet the detector. A false positive gets fixed in the script, never by editing the text back.

Functional-medicine root-cause staging is not mainstream endocrinology consensus, and the app must not imply otherwise.

- **Anything new added to the Digest follows the same structural pattern as everything already there, checked in the same pass that adds it.** Direct instruction, 2026-08-29: "Make sure that anything new added anywhere to the Digest follows the same pattern as all of the others from now on." Concretely, when adding entries to any category:
  1. **Never leave a shelf covering more than one subject undivided.** Run the real count for the shelf being added to. Past roughly 12 entries it needs named subgroups (`CONDITION_TOPIC_SUBGROUPS`, which since 2026-08-29 works for EVERY category, not just conditions: Earth Matters and Home Gardening route through `applyConditionTopicSubgroups` too, and its topic key is a plain string). - **No text ever sits directly on a tab's photographic background without a surface of its own.** Direct instruction, 2026-08-29: "No font should ever be directly on the tab background without its own background anywhere in the app." Three remedies, in order of preference: (1) a heading labelling ONE card moves INSIDE that card as its first row; (2) a heading introducing a GROUP of cards, or a standalone empty, error, loading or footnote line, gets a surface of its own (`groupHeadingChip`/`panelStandalone`, defined per screen); (3) an outline-only control gets a fill, since its label is otherwise sitting on the photo. Prefer giving the surface to the shared CONTAINER over chipping each line, so related text reads as one block rather than several stacked boxes. **`scripts/audit_bare_text_on_background.js` and `scripts/audit_dark_text_shadow.js` are the checks and both must stay at 0**, the second added 2026-09-21 after "any font that is black ... looks like a smudged font" on both the phone and Windows; a dark colour on a shadowed shared style is overridden with `textShadowColor: 'transparent', textShadowRadius: 0` rather than by splitting the style. `scripts/add_style_background.js` and `scripts/wrap_bare_text_lines.js` apply fixes at scale. Full account in `docs/CLAUDE-ARCHIVE-2026-09-14.md`.
- **Two classes of text, and only one of them scales with the phone.** Reported and asked in one message, 2026-09-18: roomier line spacing pushed the TabHub menu's bottom row of icons out through the bottom of its card, "the lower right corner box that tells you where you are needs to stay the same size no matter what the system gets changed to," and "how do we design in ways to make sure the app remains readable and usable for everything if the system phone is changed?" The answer, written up in full under "Text that is furniture, and text that is reading" in `lib/textSpacing.ts`:
  1. **READING text is everything somebody came to read**: a Digest entry, a food name, a label on a form, an advisory, a sentence in a card. It scales with the phone's font-size setting, uncapped, and its container grows with it. That is nearly the whole app, and a cap here would be the accessibility bug rather than the fix. Do not add `maxFontSizeMultiplier` to reading text, and do not add `allowFontScaling={false}` to it at all.
  2. **FURNITURE is the app's own structure**: the two popup menus (`TabHub`, `LensHub`, `MyItemsHub`), the corner box that says where you are, the version number. It is positioned rather than flowing, so it cannot grow without landing on its neighbour. Its room is BUDGETED from both inputs at once, the app's line spacing setting and the phone's font-size setting, through `menuLineHeight` and `menuLineBudget` in `constants/typography.ts`. A label's line height and the row height budgeted for it come from the same function, which is the part that stops this class of bug recurring: a hand-typed 13 next to a real 18 is exactly how the TabHub bug was written. Every label in a menu carries `maxFontSizeMultiplier={MENU_MAX_FONT_SCALE}`, and the card measures itself against that same cap through `useWindowDimensions().fontScale`, which re-renders when the setting changes where a module constant read once cannot.
  3. **PINNED is the narrow exception**: a box that truly cannot change size, meaning `PageIdentityLabel` and `VersionLabel`, both fixed against a corner by direct instruction with nowhere to grow into. Those get `allowFontScaling={false}` plus `pinnedLineHeight(size)`, which ignores the line spacing setting too. **Two files. Adding a third needs a reason written down, because ignoring the phone's setting app-wide would break the one accessibility feature that already worked.**
  `node scripts/audit_chrome_font_scale.js` is the check and must stay at 0. It walks the real TypeScript AST over the five furniture files, per-file in `cap` or `pin` mode, skips a Text nested in a Text (which inherits both attributes), and holds a `READING_TEXT_IN_CHROME` allowlist where each entry needs a written reason naming what that text grows into. Run it beside `audit_bare_text_on_background.js` before calling UI work done. Changing either line spacing or letter spacing still restarts the app, because `constants/typography.ts` applies both once at module load and every screen runs `StyleSheet.create` at import time.
- **Anything anchored to an edge is measured against the window, not against a number somebody typed.** Direct request, 2026-09-18, following the two-classes-of-text rule above, which fixed how tall the popup menus WANT to be and never asked whether the screen is that tall. It was not: `LensHub` at Normal spacing already wanted 402 dp against a 411 dp landscape phone. `useWindowDimensions()` reports dp rather than pixels, so **one measurement covers every case at once**, and there are four of them: Android's **Display size** setting (which raises the density, so the same screen reports 1.1x to 1.3x fewer dp, and which needs no second device and no rotation, making it the one most people will actually hit), a **landscape or split-screen viewport**, a **foldable**, and an **Android 16+ large screen**, which ignores an app's orientation lock outright. `app.json` sets `"orientation": "portrait"`, which narrows who sees a short viewport and does not remove it, since `"supportsTablet": true` is set too.

**Trends > Keeping Up (2026-09-23, 1.0.50.8), phase 3 of the same push.** The lens for the second audience: somebody who has capture, reminders and routines, and nothing telling them how any of it is going. Two things follow. A day with no mark is not a day undone: the app knows when somebody tapped something, not what they did, so a blank day says no mark was made and a blank week reads "nothing marked" rather than zero, carried by `renderWeekRows`'s per-caller `emptyLabel` because "not logged" is right for meals and wrong here. And a thin week is said plainly with nothing attached to it.

**Two tables were added, not one.** The plan named `routine_runs` as the only schema decision in the push, since `routines` kept `last_completed_at` and nothing else, so there was no history to chart. `upkeep_items` turned out to have the identical gap: `markUpkeepDone` overwrites the single `last_done_on`, so band 4 would have been a snapshot for good. Both tables start on 2026-09-23 with nothing behind them, so those two bands say they count from the day they started keeping every one.

**The local-day rule, which every later reader of these tables needs.** `done_check_marks.marked_at`, `capture_notes.created_at` and `routine_runs.started_at` all store `new Date().toISOString()`, which is UTC. Slicing ten characters off one gives the UTC day, so six hours west of Greenwich every evening mark lands on tomorrow and a run of evening marks reads as no run at all. `dayOf` in `lib/keepingUpDb.ts` goes through a `Date` and returns the LOCAL day, the way `describeMarkMoment` in `lib/routines.ts` always has, and each SQL window reaches a day past the range at both ends before being narrowed in JS. `upkeep_doings.done_on` and `work_checkins.week_of` are plain dates and need none of it. `lib/keepingUp.ts` is pure and holds every sentence (424 checks), `lib/keepingUpDb.ts` does the reads, and Home gained a `keepingUp` card in the Trends group: the longest run leads, with whatever is waiting under it. Still open from this phase's scope: the regulation and sensory tags becoming a band inside the existing Symptoms & Flares lens.

## Technology stack (decided)

- **React Native on Expo**, chosen over Flutter for its broader third-party ecosystem (OneDrive, Google Drive, push notifications, store billing, encrypted local storage), which matters for a solo builder.
- **Why Expo:** dev hardware is a Windows PC plus an Android phone, no Mac (a daughter's iPhone is available for occasional testing). Expo Go runs the app live on the phone with no native build step, and EAS Build compiles installable and iOS builds in the cloud, so a Mac is never required.
- **Builder's background:** 30 years as an IT Manager, some scripting, no prior mobile development, and a strong track record learning new technical domains. Building this personally with step-by-step direction and code-level help from Claude Code in VS Code.
- **Platform scope:** Android, iPhone, Windows, Mac. Desktop runs on Expo's web target configured as an installable PWA. ## Architecture decisions

*Cloud backup, sync, encryption, and household sharing are decided but **not implemented**. All app code so far is local-only and single-device. The local encrypted backup/restore and device-to-device recipe sharing that shipped in August are different, narrower features and did not become this.*

**Local-first, no company server holding health data.** The authoritative database lives on the primary account holder's device. There is no backend storing anyone's health data. This is a deliberate trust stance, not just a technical choice.

**Encrypted backup to the user's own cloud drive** (their OneDrive or Google Drive, their storage), serving as disaster recovery, multi-device sync, and household-sharing transport. Encryption is protected by a master password or a locally-generated recovery key shown once at setup. The app never holds the key, so there is intentionally **no "forgot password" flow.**

**Multi-device sync model, as designed:** the encrypted cloud snapshot is the current state rather than just a backup, pulled and decrypted locally, worked against fully offline, and pushed back. Three layered mechanisms were designed against concurrent edits (an exclusive lock held by the first device to pull and recorded in cloud metadata, an active takeover by a visible tap-required push rather than a silent one, which both mobile OSes throttle unreliably, and a hard 30-minute lease so an unreachable device can never lock somebody out), with last-write-wins plus a visible activity log underneath all three, deliberately not a CRDT, a choice validated by AnkiWeb's mature sync falling back to one-way overwrite when changes cannot merge. **What shipped instead, from 1.0.42.28, is the shared-folder snapshot sync described in the Technology stack section above, which merges three ways rather than locking.** The locking design is kept in full in `docs/CLAUDE-ARCHIVE-2026-09-14.md`, and is the step nobody has needed yet.

**Household sharing, two access tiers** reading from a shared cloud folder:

- *Free viewer companion.* Reads periodic snapshots from a natively-shared cloud folder, modeled on Anki shared decks (one-way periodic reimport, no live subscription). Sees meal schedule, shopping list, and trend reports. One narrow write exception: **checking off shopping-list items** and marking scheduled tasks done. These are idempotent, single-field, low-conflict actions sent back as a small delta file, needing no merge logic.
- *Paid interactive companion.* Read-write access to a shared "household domain" (meal plan, shopping list, schedule) but **never** the primary's personal health domain (symptoms, labs, supplements, scoring history), which stays unreachable by any companion account.

Cross-provider sharing (OneDrive on one end, Google Drive on the other) does **not** require matching providers. The sync mechanism that makes that work is deliberately deferred to its own design pass.

**Sharing between people: the `.is` format.** `.is` is intended as the one general-purpose file format for anything shareable: recipes and favorites (built), plus future gardening, exercise, and other non-health content. A person's health domain stays outside it, since that's the tier model's job. Build the payload shape generically from day one (an envelope type per content kind), not recipe-specific.

The standing security target, in the owner's words: "for data to be shared between two people who have the app, one of them had to send an invitation from the app to the other and the other had to accept it for their encrypted link to be associated to each other. If that encryption code is wrong from the person when you receive the data then it isn't accepted. Only the app knows what that code is between the two." Alongside that, a standing **Connections roster** so a repeat share doesn't need re-pairing, and OS-level `.is` file registration so tapping a received file from WhatsApp, email, or a file manager opens this app directly.

**Ordered prerequisite list for the security work** (given directly 2026-08-15; the full seven-step list with the reasoning behind each step's place in the order is in `docs/CLAUDE-ARCHIVE-2026-09-14.md`). Steps 1, 2, 3 and 5 are built, confirmed by reading the code on 2026-09-06: `tweetnacl` is a dependency, `lib/deviceIdentity.ts` keeps a private key in `expo-secure-store` (Android Keystore, iOS Keychain), `connections` stores a unique public key per person, and `decodeShareEnvelope` in `lib/sharing.ts` verifies every received payload against the verbatim signed bytes, rejecting outright on mismatch. Step 4, the invitation and pairing exchange, was one-sided and became two-way in 1.0.34.30. Step 6, `.is` OS-level file-type registration, is in `app.json`'s intent filters (content:// and file:// `.is` paths, plus the https App Link added 2026-09-14). **Step 7, extending past recipes to gardening, exercise and the rest, is still open, alongside transport**, and should be designed and built together with tier-separation work rather than as a phase beforehand.

**Reminders and scheduling: contextual from day one, not clock-based.** "Take your levothyroxine" should account for what's actually been logged, since a calcium-rich food earlier pushes the safe window later. Two mechanisms: fully local on-device scheduled notifications for anything self-contained, and a minimal **content-blind** push relay (APNs/FCM) purely for cross-device wake-up signals, never carrying health content. Background execution is throttled on both mobile OSes, so instant recomputation is only guaranteed in the foreground. Design around that. Reminders should show their own freshness ("based on food logged as of 7:45am"), because a confidently-wrong contextual reminder is worse than an honestly-uninformed static one.

**Interaction rules engine, two kinds, kept visually distinct:**

- *Built-in cited rules.* Prescription, supplement, and food timing interactions (levothyroxine with calcium or iron, biotin interfering with thyroid assays), authored with the same citation discipline as the nutrition database. Should ship as a versioned data file updatable independently of app releases.
- *Personal rule builder* (built 2026-08-18). The person's own discovered pattern or their doctor's specific instruction, always labeled distinctly.
- *Synergy to build toward.* The trend engine surfacing candidate personal rules from observed patterns: "you've logged fatigue within a day of high-goitrogen meals four times this month, want to turn that into a rule?" The Pattern Finder to rule shortcut is the natural next step and was explicitly deferred out of the 2026-08-18 build.

**Late and retroactive logging** must be low-friction: guided quick-select (what did you eat, pick from favorites or recents, when did it happen, smart time picker), never a blank form. Every late entry immediately shows its consequence (recalculated schedule impact), turning logging into a teaching moment. Scheduled reminders double as catch-up checkpoints.

**Reporting.** Doctor, nutritionist, trainer, and self reports are different views over the same timestamped, taggable log data, with every entry timestamped and taggable from the start so reporting is a query rather than a rebuild. Reports generate entirely on-device (PDF export, no server rendering), consistent with the privacy model. The laid-out PDF export shipped 2026-09-14 (1.0.37.37): one `ReportDocument` rendered two ways, plain text on screen and HTML through `expo-print` for the PDF, shared through `expo-sharing`. Doctor, nutritionist, trainer and self views over that document are not yet separate; there is one Overview report.

## Healing-journey stages

Decided 2026-07-31. Both halves (self-declaration with advisory, and reordering) shipped 2026-08-09 across 6 staged conditions: Hashimoto's, IBS, Celiac, IBD, Chronic Kidney Disease, and Gout. That is the full list of citable staged-food candidates across the 19.

Implementation: `lib/healingStage.ts`, `lib/healingStageAdvisory.ts`, `lib/conditionStages.ts`, `lib/conditionStageAdvisory.ts`, `lib/foodStageReordering.ts`, plus per-condition advisory modules, wired into all direct-ingredient Food builders, Profile, and `components/FoodLookup.tsx`.

**Framework:** the 5-stage healing and regression model (distinct from disease-progression staging), associated with Dr. Izabella Wentz: 1. Triage, 2. Digging, 3. Gut Repair, 4. Rebalancing, 5. Maintenance. Self-declared at setup, then adjusted over time by the existing Assessment. The app *suggests* a stage change and never silently reclassifies.

**What stage does: advisory and reordering only, never gating.** Stage-appropriate foods surface first, and anything questionable gets a color-coded flag linked to an explanation. Nothing is ever hidden or blocked.

**Evidence honesty.** Of the supplied sources, exactly one claim carries strong trial-level evidence: selenium reducing TPO antibodies, backed by a meta-analysis of 21 RCTs. Everything else is practitioner framework, case report, or survey data. The thiamine claim is a **3-patient case report**, not the "landmark clinical pilot trial" it was described as; gluten, dairy, and soy trigger rankings come from one practitioner's self-reported audience survey; and the staging framework itself is its own author explaining it. That is fine to build on **provided the app labels it honestly.** The full source table with per-claim tiers is in the archive.

**Practical scoping:** only stages 2 and 3 meaningfully drive *food* decisions. Stages 1, 4, and 5 are about hormone dosing, nutrient correction, adaptogens, sleep, and labs. Five stages are right for education, but builder rules will key off fewer distinctions.

## The Unified Whole-Foods Database

`unified-database/` is a separate SQLite database (`unified_foods.sqlite`, ~115MB) and Node.js pipeline started 2026-08-10, built to become a richer, fully-auditable successor to `assets/data/foods_reference.db`. **Not merged, not wired to the app in any way.** `lib/db.ts` never reads from it and no screen touches it, so it is safe to iterate on indefinitely with zero risk to the live app.

**Why:** the current reference DB is the output of a one-time ad hoc Python filter script whose reasoning was never captured as testable rules. The unified pipeline rebuilds it reproducibly, discarding nothing (every original record survives in `raw_foods.raw_json`), classifying by a versioned unit-tested rule engine, matching across sources by species, Latin-name, and LanguaL evidence rather than fuzzy name guessing, and reviewing human-in-the-loop via a published audit tool.

**Architecture:** `sources` (9 registered) feeds `raw_foods`, which feeds `raw_food_nutrients` and `whole_food_classifications` (with a `reviewed` flag protecting human overrides from automated re-runs), which feed `food_match_groups` and `food_match_members`. One script per pipeline stage lives in `pipeline/`, each with a DB-backed test suite (391 passing). One adapter per source lives in `sources/`, so adding source #10 means registering it plus writing one adapter.

**Status (this drifts, so verify via `sqlite3.exe unified_foods.sqlite` rather than trusting these numbers):** all 9 sources ingested, classified, and matched. 32,707 raw records, of which 16,661 are classified whole-food, 13,093 are not, and 2,953 are unresolved. 14,521 match groups. The audit tool is live at https://claude.ai/code/artifact/51c33d40-cbd9-4468-90b0-e1e460fd5b1d, and review has started (242 classification decisions applied) but is nowhere near complete.

**Phases:** 1 (schema and pipeline) and 2 (ingestion) are done. **3 and 4 (human review) have tooling done but review barely started, and this is the standing next step.** 5 (merge the app's curated D1-D6 and condition scores, aliases, and interaction rules onto the new master rather than re-deriving them), 6 (automated integrity verification), and 7 (the atomic reversible swap, gated on `REFERENCE_DB_VERSION`) are not started. Nothing here is urgent or blocking.

**If picking this up fresh:** read `unified-database/README.md` in full first.

## Named, unresolved risks (product, not engineering)

1. **Logging discipline and retention.** The single biggest risk to the whole vision is whether a person will log consistently enough, over weeks and months, for contextual reminders and trend correlation to mean anything. Most health-tracking apps fail here, not on missing features. Worth testing this assumption cheaply before building the full data model around it.
2. **Signal quality.** Whether symptom and food correlation-finding on noisy, confounded, n=1 personal data surfaces real patterns versus things that merely look like patterns. Must be handled honestly in-product, showing confidence and never asserting causation.
