## What this app is

**Inside Story** was chosen after ruling out Aurelia, Chrysalis, Monarch, Second Nature, Food Sense, FoodFlex, and Backstory, each of which had a collision. It is an English idiom, not an invented word.

**Never describe this as a Hashimoto's app, or even only an autoimmune-conditions app.** The creator's framing: "everything has an inside story… This app started to help with Hashimoto's, then decided to help with 18 other conditions, then has grown from there to include basically everything in life revolving around a person." Describe it as an everything-about-a-person's-life app that started with, and runs deepest on, food and autoimmune health. Garden, budget, family, soil microbiome, and the body are all in scope.

**Scope, stated directly:** "this is no longer just focused on Hashimoto's, it is focused on all 19 both individually and if a person has multiple of them combined." Every one of the 18 non-Hashimoto's conditions is a standing target for the same depth, not a lesser tier. Combined-condition (polyautoimmunity) management is its own named scope item, not yet built as a first-class destination.

**Who it is for, past the 19 conditions.** Direct statement, 2026-09-16: "I would like for this app to not just be viewed as being for the 19 conditions. The Autism and ADHD additions don’t much have anything to do with food at all, but the app itself is the thing that will help people who do have those neurodivergent traits to cope with daily living and all of the details that do need to be kept track of and reminding them of and making sure they have a way to keep all of those things from being on their minds. That in itself, I think will be a boon for them."

So there is a second audience with as strong a claim on this app as the health one: someone who needs an outside place to hold the details of daily living, so those details stop taking up room in their head. ADHD and autism are the named cases, and the need is wider than that (anyone recovering, grieving, caregiving, or simply carrying too much). For that person the point of the app is not the food scoring at all. It is Life, Schedules, reminders, and capture. The health depth is there when they want it, not the price of entry.

Two things follow, and they change what gets built:

1. **A feature that serves only the 19 conditions is a narrower feature than one that also serves this.** When both are within reach for the same effort, build both.
2. **The way in must not require a condition.** Onboarding, the Free tier, and the store description all still lead with health conditions. Someone who came for help running their day should recognize the app as theirs on the first screen. Not built, and a named gap.

**Never frame this as diet or nutrition treating ADHD or autism.** Nutrition supports them; it does not treat them, which is the line the Digest’s own ADHD entries already take. The daily-living support stands on its own and needs no food claim attached to it.

### The core purpose (read before any scope trade-off)

Help someone with an autoimmune condition relearn how and what to eat, and understand, personally and for their own body, how food affects them. Meal building, shopping lists, and scheduling are scaffolding that make daily use sustainable enough to generate that understanding. They are not the point. **When in doubt about priority, favor whatever helps someone discover and act on their own personal food-effect patterns over convenience features.**

Gut and microbiome healing is an explicit goal, not a side effect. The app is meant to actively help repair gut health, not just avoid trigger foods.

**Food first, and a supplement only for what food cannot supply.** Direct statement, 2026-09-22: "the goal is to modify how a person eats so with their food they are receiving an optimal amount of every one of them and not have to take any supplements, but to also make sure that you do take the ones you cannot receive from your food because of whatever reason, such as being vegan, or alergic, etc." A target reached by food and the same target reached by a capsule are two different outcomes, so everywhere the app shows a nutrient amount it says which one happened, and neither half of that is advice to stop taking anything. Built in four places: Insights > Nutrients, Home's Today's Fuel Gauges, My Meds at the moment a supplement is added, and Trends > Nutrients. **The supplement half is always worked out per day from each supplement's start and end dates** (`getSupplementNutrientTotalsByDate`, `lib/supplementWindow.ts`), never from today's regimen stretched backwards, which read a supplement started yesterday as having been there in June. Nothing in this app records a dose being swallowed, so where a person gave no dates the supplement is counted across the range and the caption says so.

**When a supplement is taken belongs to scheduling, and it matters.** Same statement: supplements carry rules about with or without food, with water or with fat, not with certain foods, and not with other vitamins, minerals, acids and hormones they compete with, synergistic and antagonistic both. Those rules live in the interaction-rules engine and the dose timeline in Schedules > Meds. Schedules > Today's Meals is the day itself, meals and doses interleaved by the clock, and an opened dose reads the meals around it (`lib/doseMealTiming.ts` checks a dose against the nearest qualifying MEAL, which `lib/interactionRules.ts` never did). Two things stay true whatever gets built on top: `MEAL_AMOUNT_THAT_MATTERS` is a set of working thresholds rather than doses from a trial, which is why every sentence it generates names the amount itself rather than asking to be trusted, and a meal with nothing resolvable to the food database reads as could not be checked, never as carrying nothing. Nothing on that lens writes; times, taken, skipped and removed stay on Meds, so one place holds that record.

The user's reframing: people are overwhelmed and confused about food and can't put the pieces together themselves. The app's job is to remove that cognitive burden entirely, doing the correlation and discovery work *for* the person so daily eating becomes following clear, pre-solved rules. The complexity lives inside the app; what the person experiences is ease. **This makes discovery and trend-finding something the app does on the user's behalf, not a task handed to them.**

### Living with nature, not against it (standing principle, 2026-09-26)

Direct statement: "I think the app is also about how we humans live with nature. Working with it and not against by thinking we know better than nature." This was already true in the build before it was written down: gut healing as tending an ecosystem, soil fed by compost and cover crops rather than turned and dosed, no-dig beds, the Three Sisters, the symbiosis entry in Horticulture, and food first with a supplement only for the gap. It now governs choices everywhere:

1. **When the app describes two ways of doing something, the one that works with a living system comes first and gets the fuller treatment**: the gut, soil, a garden, a hive, a body's rhythms. The other way is still described fairly, never mocked.
2. **The principle never outranks evidence tiering.** Where nature's way is losing (untreated varroa mites killing colonies, a condition that needs its medication), the app says so plainly. Working with nature includes saying where it needs help, and nothing here ever becomes advice to refuse a treatment.
3. **What a person keeps alive is recorded as a relationship over time, not an output to maximise.** A harvest is what was given, a colony or a soil is tracked for how it is doing, and no screen scores a living thing for productivity.
4. **Native and local come before imported** when both are within reach: local seed, adapted queens, native pollinators, a region's own traditional practices.

The first named consequence is **bees and beekeeping, all of it**, which has to come into the app. Direct correction, 2026-09-26: "It is also the hive log. Everything, not just some things about bees." That is a complete hive log in Garden (colonies, queens, inspections, mite counts and treatments, feeding, harvests, swarms, losses, equipment, costs, and native and stingless bee boxes, on the existing area, equipment, readings and yield patterns) and complete reading on every kind of bee and every part of keeping them, with bee-centred beekeeping set beside conventional practice, each described fairly. The full scope is the Project Tracker row [Bees and beekeeping](https://app.notion.com/p/3e753652f272816bac9ae60e053c4d6d). The owner intends to keep bees. Not designed or built yet.

### What the app gives back in the end: a life's Inside Story (2026-09-26)

Direct statement: "I've often thought about how cool it would be if people had a way to leave something behind about them that humanity would carry forward so that everyone's individual life would carry meaning through their lived life and what it looked like through their own eyes, through their own experiences. This is, in my mind, sort of the culmination of what this app gives back in the end." So the stated end point is a person's life, told through their own eyes from what they recorded over years, that they can leave to the people they choose and, if they wish, to archives built to last. It is where Your Story ends up, and its first edition already has a section called The Inside Story. Not built, and most of what it rests on (the Diary, "If something happens to me", the family tiers, years of sync) is not built either; the full account is the Decision row [A life's Inside Story](https://app.notion.com/p/3e753652f2728158b2a1dd56d6ac4a7f). Rules that bind everything built before it:

1. **The words are the person's.** The app gathers, lays out and prompts, and never writes the story in its own voice.
2. **It outlives the app.** A printed book or PDF plus an open-format archive readable with nothing installed, so choose export formats with that in mind from now on.
3. **Private until chosen.** Composing a legacy is a separate, deliberate act of picking what goes in; no record, and above all no diary entry, becomes part of it quietly. Other people named in an entry are shown before it is included.
4. **No company server holds it**, and giving anything to the public is said plainly to be permanent before the last step. Donating health data to research is a separate choice, never bundled in.

### International scope

Not US-centric. Three distinct components:

1. **Cited international and regional epidemiological data per condition:** how prevalence and severity vary by country or region, for all 19 conditions, with cited corroborating explanation (genetics, diet, environment, latitude, screening practices). One "Global Perspective" entry per condition, in progress.
2. **i18n architecture, decided but not built.** Languages: all common English variants (US, UK, AU), Spanish (Latin American and Spain), German, French, Finnish, Norwegian, Swedish, Japanese. **Chinese and Italian were removed 2026-08-10 by direct instruction.** No i18n library is wired in: no `expo-localization`, no `react-i18next`, no string extraction has been done. This is substantial engineering work, deliberately not half-started.
3. **Country-specific reference data.** The 22,022-food database already draws on 7 national sources (US, Canada, UK, Germany, Australia, France, Japan). Gaps: no Spanish-speaking country's data, and no Nordic source yet.

## Standing rules

These are behavior-changing and apply to every session:

- **The Rule Engine, a standing name for a recurring gap.** Direct request, 2026-08-26, after the oxalate/calcium-pairing work below: "we need a word for this part of the app so we both know what we are talking about... it has to always apply to all of the ways the information needs to be wired up, so nothing ever gets left out." **The Rule Engine is every mechanism in this app that encodes one evidence-based nutrition or health fact as a reusable "if X then Y" rule about a food, ingredient, nutrient or combination**, as opposed to the Digest's long-form narrative writing. It is not one file or one table; it is a set of already-built pieces that all have to be checked whenever a new fact enters the app:
  1. **Reference-database scoring** (`sub_criteria`/`food_scores`, `assets/data/foods_reference.db`): does this fact need a scored sub-criterion of its own on individual foods?
  2. **Condition relevance** (`sub_criterion_condition_relevance`): which of the 19 tracked conditions does this scored property actually matter for? (See the 2026-08-24 Migraine relevance-mapping precedent, `scripts/add_migraine_condition_relevance.js`, for the safe, established way to extend this.)
  3. **Recipe condition-cautions** (`scripts/compute_recipe_condition_data.js`, feeding `lib/digest/recipes.ts`): does a flagged recipe need a caution surfaced under "Meals You Can Eat," and is the SEVERITY calibrated (see the 2026-08-26 Oxalate Level/Oxalate Load Rank fix below for what happens when a raw measurement gets used as if it were the verdict)?
  4. **The meal generator's rule sets** (`lib/dailyMealPlan.ts`: `NUTRIENT_SYNERGY_RULES`, `NUTRIENT_ANTAGONISM_RULES`, `FREQUENCY_RULES`, and the pairing-requirement mechanism below): should this fact actively shape what gets recommended or flagged, not just described?
  5. **The interaction-rules engine** (`interaction_rules` table, `lib/interactionRules.ts`): is this a timing/combination caution between a food, supplement, and/or prescription, distinct from a per-food score?
  6. **Digest condition-specific content**: does a tracked condition this fact is relevant to deserve a cited research entry of its own explaining why?
  7. **Digest Basic Health content**: is this a general-population-relevant fact worth an entry of its own regardless of which conditions someone tracks?
  8. **Healing-stage advisories** (`lib/*StageAdvisory.ts`): does this matter differently at different stages of a staged condition's healing journey?

  Not every new fact needs all eight; a purely narrative finding (a documentary fact-check) may only need 6-7, and a pure food property may only need 1-3. The discipline is running through the list deliberately every time, not defaulting to "just write the Digest entry" and calling it done. Update this list itself if a new kind of wiring point gets built.

- **One distance between stacked bands, everywhere: `HOME_BAND_GAP` in `components/HomeSectionBand.tsx`.** Direct instruction, 2026-09-12: "Set that distance as the standard for distance between them on every part of the app." Every scrolling screen in the app takes the band look as of 2026-09-19: surfaces run edge to edge with a left accent in the tab's colour, `makeTabBandStyles(tabColor)` in `components/TabBand.tsx` hands a tab its card and heading bands, and any screen reads `HOME_BAND_GAP` for its column gap and for any band's gap to what follows it, never a retyped 10 or 14. The same applies to a band's inner content inset, `HOME_BAND_CONTENT_PADDING`, which is also the side margin of any button standing between bands. Scroll content carries no side padding and no bottom margin: the bottom padding comes inline from `useFloatingButtonScrollPadding`, a full window of run-out, so a background photo the person chose shows through past the last band. Bare lead, footnote and empty-state text is wrapped in a band rather than sitting on the photo. `homeBandStyle` carries View-only props, so it never goes on a `Text` directly.
- **The Digest owns full content; the tool areas own data.** Direct instruction, 2026-08-18: "Think of the Digest as being where we pass along full read knowledge and the rest of the areas are for collecting data, manipulating it, and reporting it in meaningful ways." The rule outlives the tab: since 2026-09-19 the reading lenses (Conditions, Health Literacy, Earth Matters on Life; Horticulture on Garden) are where full content lives. Tool-area screens use compact rows (one line of primary text, one muted caption); full descriptions open on tap via `showInfoAlert()`, never sitting permanently on screen. Data-entry forms are the exception and stay as `formCard`s.
- **Never frame Hashimoto's as more central than the other 18 tracked conditions** (memory key `feedback_no_hashimotos_primacy_framing`).
- **Home cooking over commercial and branded products.** Don't expand branded or commercial reference entries. The intended path for a store-bought item is the barcode scan, not a generic "commercial" or "préemballée" entry.
- **Writing style in app content, and in this document:** avoid the filler words "real," "genuine," and "genuinely." **No dashes used in place of ordinary punctuation:** no em dashes, no en dashes, and no " -- " double-hyphen pattern. Rewrite with a colon, a comma, parentheses, or a second sentence instead. Hyphens in ordinary compound words (self-declared, on-device, read-write) are fine. **No redundant "own"** in constructions like "this category's own research," "the trial's own authors," "glyphosate's own cancer classification," the possessive already establishes ownership, "own" adds nothing and isn't how people actually talk (2026-08-24 direct correction, "This is not how people talk... The word 'own' has no place there at all"). A rare, genuinely legitimate use survives: the "in its own right"/"on its own terms"/"on its own merits" idiom family, the standalone "on X's own" (independently) idiom, and the real self-vs-other medical framing in "attacks the body's own tissue." Everything else gets the "own" removed.

  **This rule is checkable now, so stop checking it by memory.** `node scripts/audit_ai_isms.js [paths]` walks a character-level scanner over `lib`, `app`, `components`, `constants` and `hooks`, separating string literals, JSX text runs, SQL template literals and code comments, and reports hits in 13 categories at two tiers: `block` (fix it) and `look` (a person decides). Run it on anything you wrote before calling content work done.

  **`.claude/agents/ai-ism-editor.md` is the agent that does the removing.** Direct instruction, 2026-09-18: it runs on newly written or newly changed content only, never on a schedule and never on content nobody asked about, and its start and completion are announced every time. It reports a per-category tally with the before and after text of every change, because the tally is as much the deliverable as the edits. Claude Code reads `.claude/agents/` once, at session start, so an agent written during a session is missing from that session; the fix is to restart Claude Code, never to substitute a different agent, since the standing rule everywhere else is no subagents on this project.

  **Four standing consequences of the 2026-09-18 sweeps, each of which a later session will be tempted to undo:**
  1. **A Digest title says what the entry is about.** Direct instruction: "The title of something shouldn't make someone still wonder what they are about to read." The shape that was removed is a subject, a colon, and a phrase that promises rather than states ("Water: The One Nutrient With No Official Government Recommendation at All"), and 116 titles were rewritten to name the subject and the finding. The colon-subtitle convention was explicitly rejected as house style, so do not reintroduce it, and do not write a new entry with one.
  2. **The `stageNote` field is gone from `DigestEntry` and from the renderer.** All 205 of them were deleted by direct instruction. They were small grey italic notes explaining why an entry had been included, which is curation rationale and belongs in a code comment. Do not add the field back and do not put the same content in `teaser` or `summary` instead.
  3. **The "already covered" cross-reference refrain is out of customer-facing text.** A bare self-reference ("already covered in this category") was deleted outright, since it points at nothing a reader can go to. A pointer naming a place a reader can actually reach was kept, minus the word "already", which presumes the reader has been through it. The 27 survivors are all in code comments, which is where curation rationale belongs.
  4. **`OWNER_CHOICE` in `scripts/audit_ai_isms.js` holds two deliberate exemptions**, "Industry, Greenwashing & Honest Limits" and "Building Real Soil". Both were asked for back by name after a sweep had shortened them to quiet the detector. A false positive gets fixed in the script, never by editing the text back.
- **Evidence tiering is non-negotiable.** Every claim carries an honest evidence tier. A personal hypothesis must never be presented as verified medical fact, including in any doctor-facing report. Functional-medicine root-cause staging is not mainstream endocrinology consensus, and the app must not imply otherwise.
- **What the app must never say about the person (2026-09-24, Phase A of the gap review).** Five things, checked by `node scripts/audit_clinical_claims.js`, which must stay at 0: (1) never diagnose; (2) never claim a cause from the person's own records, since Pattern Finder, Trends and Therapy Response see a sample of one and "tends to follow" is the most they can say; (3) never tell anybody to stop, start, skip or change a medication or its dose, where timing advice from the interaction rules is fine and the prescription is the prescriber's; (4) never call something not an emergency, or a symptom nothing to worry about; (5) never let a score stand in for a clinician. The check reads the modules that write sentences about the person (every `*Advisory.ts`, the interaction rules, pattern, trend, therapy and dose timing generators, the report, the emergency card), not the reading corpus. A sentence carrying its own "do not" or "talk to your prescriber" passes; anything else intended goes in its `ALLOWED` map with a reason. Add a new sentence generator to its `NAMED` list when one is built.
- **Pattern Finder says what it is counting against (2026-09-24, Phase B of the gap review).** Every count names its denominators, flares with no meals logged before them are left out and said so, and each candidate is compared with how often the same food turns up in any ordinary stretch of the same length, cut from the same meal list at 6, 12, 18 and 24 o'clock (`lib/patternBasis.ts`). Sleep and treatment changes around the same flares are listed beside the foods and never offered as the explanation (`lib/patternContext.ts`). "Test this" opens a leave-it-out-then-bring-it-back experiment in Signals, whose result compares before, without and back and always ends with its one-run limit (`lib/foodExperiment.ts`, the `design`, `removal_started_on`, `removal_days` and `measure` columns on `food_trials`); a waiting experiment is not started by a meal eaten during its days without the food. Weight, steps, sleep and labs on Trends carry "your usual range", the middle 80% of the earlier readings shown, and the words say it is what readings have been, never what they should be (`lib/yourUsual.ts`). `node scripts/test_phase_b_patterns.js` covers all four and sweeps them for verdict words.

Concretely, when adding entries to any category:
  1. **Never leave a shelf covering more than one subject undivided.** Count the shelf being added to. Past roughly 12 entries it needs named subgroups (`CONDITION_TOPIC_SUBGROUPS`, which since 2026-08-29 works for EVERY category, not just conditions, and whose topic key is a plain string). - **Two classes of text, and only one of them scales with the phone.** Reported and asked in one message, 2026-09-18: roomier line spacing pushed the TabHub menu's bottom row of icons out through the bottom of its card, "the lower right corner box that tells you where you are needs to stay the same size no matter what the system gets changed to," and "how do we design in ways to make sure the app remains readable and usable for everything if the system phone is changed?" The answer, written up in full under "Text that is furniture, and text that is reading" in `lib/textSpacing.ts`:
  1. **READING text is everything somebody came to read**: a Digest entry, a food name, a label on a form, an advisory, a sentence in a card. It scales with the phone's font-size setting, uncapped, and its container grows with it. That is nearly the whole app, and a cap here would be the accessibility bug rather than the fix. Do not add `maxFontSizeMultiplier` to reading text, and do not add `allowFontScaling={false}` to it at all.
  2. **FURNITURE is the app's structure**: the two popup menus (`TabHub`, `LensHub`, `MyItemsHub`), the corner box that says where you are, the version number. It is positioned rather than flowing, so it cannot grow without landing on its neighbour. Its room is BUDGETED from both inputs at once, the app's line spacing setting and the phone's font-size setting, through `menuLineHeight` and `menuLineBudget` in `constants/typography.ts`. A label's line height and the row height budgeted for it come from the same function, which is what stops this class of bug recurring: a hand-typed 13 next to an 18 is exactly how the TabHub bug was written. Every label in a menu carries `maxFontSizeMultiplier={MENU_MAX_FONT_SCALE}`, and the card measures itself against that cap through `useWindowDimensions().fontScale`, which re-renders when the setting changes where a module constant read once cannot.
  3. **PINNED is the narrow exception**: a box that truly cannot change size, meaning `PageIdentityLabel` and `VersionLabel`, both fixed against a corner by direct instruction with nowhere to grow into. Those get `allowFontScaling={false}` plus `pinnedLineHeight(size)`, which ignores the line spacing setting too. **Two files. Adding a third needs a reason written down, because ignoring the phone's setting app-wide would break the one accessibility feature that already worked.**
  `node scripts/audit_chrome_font_scale.js` is the check and must stay at 0. It walks the TypeScript AST over the five furniture files, per-file in `cap` or `pin` mode, skips a Text nested in a Text (which inherits both attributes), and holds a `READING_TEXT_IN_CHROME` allowlist where each entry needs a written reason naming what that text grows into. Run it beside `audit_bare_text_on_background.js` before calling UI work done. Changing either line spacing or letter spacing restarts the app, because `constants/typography.ts` applies both once at module load and every screen runs `StyleSheet.create` at import time.
- **Anything anchored to an edge is measured against the window, not against a number somebody typed.** Direct request, 2026-09-18, after the two-classes-of-text rule fixed how tall the popup menus WANT to be and never asked whether the screen is that tall (`LensHub` at Normal spacing wanted 402 dp against a 411 dp landscape phone). `useWindowDimensions()` reports dp rather than pixels, so **one measurement covers every case at once**: Android's **Display size** setting (which raises the density, needs no second device and no rotation, and is the one most people will actually hit), a **landscape or split-screen viewport**, a **foldable**, and an **Android 16+ large screen**, which ignores an app's orientation lock outright. `"orientation": "portrait"` in `app.json` narrows who sees a short viewport and does not remove it.
  - The arithmetic lives in `lib/menuFit.ts` (`menuCardRoom`, `fitMenuCard`), pure and with no React in it so `node scripts/test_menuFit.js` can check it without a phone, and `useMenuCardFit(desired, minHeight)` in `constants/floatingButton.ts` is the hook every menu calls. **A clamped card scrolls**: all three menus put their content through a `ScrollView` unconditionally, because a `ScrollView` shorter than its frame simply does not scroll, so there is one code path to be right about rather than two. `node scripts/audit_menu_card_fit.js` is the check and must stay at 0: every menu file calls `useMenuCardFit`, and a hand-typed `height`, `maxHeight` or `minHeight` on a `card*` style is rejected. `PageIdentityLabel` was checked and deliberately not changed.
- **Lists are open-ended, and removing an entry never orphans or destroys a record.** Direct instruction, 2026-09-20: "we need to make it as future-proof as possible by giving ways to add things in lists that we haven't foreseen the need for, or the existence of," and, on removal, "I don't think anything should be orphaned if the user deletes a field label they created. That isn't good database management." Two halves. **Five rules the push settled, each of which a later session will be tempted to soften.**

1. **A lens is a SUBJECT holding several bands behind the fold**, the way Nutrients holds every nutrient, which is what took roughly twenty proposed charts down to 14 lenses. The record lives on its tab and the timeline lives on Trends, so nothing on Trends writes.
2. **A period with nothing logged draws as a gap, never as a zero.** Being too busy to log is not a week of eating nothing, so every periodic figure is `number | null`, a blank period says so in words (`renderWeekRows` takes an `emptyLabel` per caller, since "not logged" is right for meals and "nothing marked" is right for a day somebody tapped nothing), and the count of blank periods sits under the headline. It also rules out a line chart for a gapped series, since a line either joins across the blank, inventing a period, or plots it at zero.
3. **The local-day rule, which every later reader of these tables needs.** `done_check_marks.marked_at`, `capture_notes.created_at` and `routine_runs.started_at` store `new Date().toISOString()`, which is UTC, so slicing ten characters off one puts every evening mark west of Greenwich on tomorrow and reads a run of evening marks as no run at all. `dayOf` in `lib/keepingUpDb.ts` goes through a `Date` for the LOCAL day, the way `describeMarkMoment` in `lib/routines.ts` always has, and each SQL window reaches a day past the range at both ends before being narrowed in JS. Plain date columns (`upkeep_doings.done_on`, `work_checkins.week_of`, `harvest_uses.used_on`, every garden table) need none of it.
4. **Units are never converted across kinds.** Grams, kilos, ounces and pounds add through `convertToGrams`; a count of cucumbers is a count and never becomes a weight; anything else (bunches, buckets) stays on a line of its own, which is also why `COMPOST_AMOUNT_UNITS` totals stay per unit. So a percentage or a price is only ever taken of a weight, and a crop somebody counts rather than weighs carries no share beside it.
5. **Only money recorded against a day is counted, and a figure is never guessed at.** `finance_recurring` rules are left out, since drawing a history out of a rule about what should happen every month would invent spending in months nobody entered any. There is no link column between `finance_medical_bills` and `finance_entries`, so anything entered in both places is counted from both and the note under the band states that rather than pretending to de-duplicate. Money on a harvest is worked out only from prices the person has recorded paying for the same food in the same unit, is an avoided cost rather than income, and says both. And band 4 of What It Costs never says a supplement was unnecessary, only that a target is now being met by food, which is the stated goal.

**Growing conditions as a record, and the sensor plan behind it (2026-09-23, 1.0.50.12).** The idea a later session will be tempted to skip past: **separate the reading from the radio.** A soil moisture figure is worth the same whether it arrived over WiFi or came off a $12 meter, so the record gets built first and every way of filling it is a later, optional addition. Four stages were proposed and **only stage 0 is approved and built**: stage 1 is DIY LAN sensors over the zeroconf and static-server code already compiled in, stage 2 cameras over RTSP or ONVIF (a native module and a rebuild), stage 3 commercial cloud accounts over OAuth. Three corrections to the outside advice that started this bind whatever gets built later: **no time-series database**, since SQLite handles a sensor's rate and a second engine breaks local-first; **no Flutter and no Node or Python backend**, since Expo is settled and a backend is the one thing this app deliberately lacks; and **nothing is built on Home Assistant**, since requiring a hub disqualifies the audience. The phone is the hub.

Stage 0 is `garden_readings`, append-only on the `harvest_uses` precedent, with no foreign key, `plot_name` on the row so a Past Area keeps its readings readable, and a `source` of `hand` or `device` that exists now so stage 1 needs no migration. `measurement_kind` is the fifth open list and the first that is not movable: readings are history, so a measurement name with readings against it retires rather than asking for a reassignment. **Units are turned into one another only where the two mean the same quantity** (°C and °F, mm and in, L and gal, mS/cm and µS/cm); a moisture percentage and a tensiometer centibar are deliberately not a family, nor are lux and PPFD, so readings in the odd unit out are set aside with a line saying how many and in what. Rain and water given are added up over a month, everything else averaged with its lowest and highest, since an average soil temperature hides the night that dropped below freezing. The forbidden-words sweep in `scripts/test_growing_conditions.js` adds "too low", "too high", "ideal", "optimal" and "healthy range", since a conditions figure is the easiest place in this app to start telling somebody their soil is wrong.

## Technology stack (decided)

- **React Native on Expo**, chosen over Flutter for its broader third-party ecosystem (OneDrive, Google Drive, push notifications, store billing, encrypted local storage), which matters for a solo builder.
- **Why Expo:** dev hardware is a Windows PC plus an Android phone, no Mac (a daughter's iPhone is available for occasional testing). Expo Go runs the app live on the phone with no native build step, and EAS Build compiles installable and iOS builds in the cloud, so a Mac is never required.
- **Builder's background:** 30 years as an IT Manager, some scripting, no prior mobile development, and a strong track record learning new technical domains. Building this personally with step-by-step direction and code-level help from Claude Code in VS Code.
- **Platform scope:** Android, iPhone, Windows, Mac. Desktop runs on Expo's web target configured as an installable PWA. Windows ships through PWABuilder to MSIX to the Microsoft Store (no platform fee); Mac is a browser-installed PWA, deliberately avoiding Apple's $99/year Developer Program and notarization.
- **Config plugins:** `plugins/withAutofillDisabled.js` and `plugins/withWindowBackground.js` are the established pattern for native config changes Expo has no first-class field for.
- **The desktop app is the same code in an Electron window (`desktop/`, since 2026-09-21, 1.0.42.16).** Direct decision: "I would rather have a desktop app instead of a web app or use via a web browser," then "Electron is fine, write it up and start on step 1." Step 1 (the shell and installer) and step 2 (carrying a phone's data over, both directions) are built; step 3 is an Open row in the Project Tracker. **The full account of every mechanism below, and the bug behind each rule, is in `docs/CLAUDE-ARCHIVE-2026-09-14.md`. Read it there before re-deciding any of this.** What stands:
  - **How it is built.** `node desktop/build-web.js` runs `expo export -p web` with `INSIDE_STORY_DESKTOP=1`, which makes `metro.config.js` resolve expo-sqlite, expo-secure-store, expo-notifications and expo-file-system to the stand-ins in `lib/desktop/` and the phone-only natives to `unavailableModule.ts`. `desktop/main.js` does what those natives did: SQLite on Node's built-in `node:sqlite`, secrets through `safeStorage`, reminders as timers, files under `%APPDATA%/inside-story-desktop/`, the export served over a private `app://inside-story/` scheme, and the reference database shipped beside the app (`extraResources`). `preload.js` exposes it as `window.insideStoryDesktop`, `lib/desktop/bridge.ts` describes that object, and `isDesktopApp()` there is the check the app uses. **The phone bundle is untouched**: the flag is never set for it and `@expo/fingerprint` does not hash `metro.config.js`. `cd desktop && npm run dist` builds the installer, and `buildVersion` in `electron-builder.yml` bumps with the app.

Do not re-add it. On web `app/(tabs)/_layout.tsx` calls `enableScreens(true)` at module level, without which every visited tab stays drawn under the current one, and a tap on another hub's button while a menu is open lands on that hub through `lib/hubHandoff.ts`.

The two file dialogs do work (`desktop/files.js`), so an exported backup, a PDF report and a shared `.is` file land where the person chooses. `Directory.pickDirectoryAsync` still says not available.
  - **On a computer OneDrive is the folder on the disk, and there is no sign-in** (1.0.42.25), because the phone's Graph sign-in ends at a `hashimotosapp://` redirect nothing on a PC is registered for. `lib/oneDriveAuth.ts` reports signed in on desktop and `lib/oneDriveGraph.ts` sends every call to `lib/desktop/cloudFolder.ts`, which addresses a folder as `driveId 'disk'` with the absolute path as its `itemId`, so nothing in `lib/db.ts`, `lib/syncInbox.ts` or the backup code changed. `desktop/cloudFolder.js` writes to a temp file then renames, so the OneDrive client never uploads half a backup, and refuses a write outside a detected root, the home folder, or a folder picked through the OS dialog this run. **A folder travels between the two kinds of device by its sentence**, "OneDrive / Apps / Inside Story" (`folderFromPhonePath`, `folderFromComputerPath`).
  - **One person's two devices stay in step through the shared folder (1.0.42.28 onward).** Each device keeps one encrypted copy of its whole database in the Backups folder, replaced on every save, beside a plaintext record, `inside-story-sync.json`, which says which device saved last and when and carries no health content; manual `inside-story-backup-*` files are untouched. It is turned on in Profile > Backup & Restore with one password, the same on both devices. `lib/snapshotSync.ts` holds every decision with no I/O, `lib/snapshotSyncDevice.ts` reads, writes and restores, `lib/databaseActivity.ts` counts row-changing statements, and `components/SnapshotSyncWatcher.tsx` saves about fifteen seconds after the last write and checks the folder at startup, on foreground, on desktop window focus and every half minute. **Four things hold, each bought with a bug:**
    1. **Some rows and some tables never travel.** `DEVICE_LOCAL_META_KEYS` in `lib/snapshotSync.ts` lists the `app_meta` rows a device keeps to itself, and `DEVICE_LOCAL_TABLES` does the same for whole tables (it holds `sync_change_log`). **Add a key to that list whenever a new `app_meta` row addresses hardware, a path, a permission or a bundled file, and never assume a row is safe because it looks like a setting**, which is what carrying `onedrive_folder` over taught: the phone inherited the computer's Windows path, could reach no folder, and quietly published nothing for a day.
    2. **Every arrival is a three-way merge, record by record** (`lib/snapshotMerge.ts`, which imports nothing): base, here and there, decided per row, settled by the time the row itself carries (`TIME_COLUMNS`) and falling back to whichever device saved later, with the losing change written down as a clash rather than vanishing. Row shapes are read from the schema at merge time, so a table added later merges without anybody listing it. Three corrections stand: **the agreed copy is what the OTHER device last sent, never what this one published**; **a merge marks the device unsaved only when it has something to send** (`sendsBack`, without which two devices left open answered each other every half minute, and why the two fold maps moved to the device-local `visual_fold_state`); and **Sync Now never forces**, since forcing would put this device's rows where both devices' rows belong.
    3. **What changed is said in words, and the words carry no health content out of the encryption.** `lib/snapshotChanges.ts` reduces the stripped tables to one `{n, h}` per table and diffs two stamps into phrases through a map of roughly 50 areas, a count coming from one table per area so a salad and its six ingredient rows read as "1 more salad", four phrases at most, then "N other things". **The list rides INSIDE the encrypted snapshot**, never in the plaintext record, since what somebody added to is as much their health as what they added.
    4. **Nothing is said out loud, and the log is where a merge is read.** `ANNOUNCE_MERGES` in `lib/snapshotSync.ts` is a constant set to false, read by both `lib/snapshotSyncDevice.ts` and `lib/peerSyncDevice.ts`, so turning the notices back on is that one line. ## Architecture decisions

*Cloud backup, sync, encryption, and household sharing are decided but **not implemented**. All app code so far is local-only and single-device. The local encrypted backup/restore and device-to-device recipe sharing that shipped in August are different, narrower features and did not become this.*

**Local-first, no company server holding health data.** The authoritative database lives on the primary account holder's device. There is no backend storing anyone's health data. This is a deliberate trust stance, not just a technical choice.

**Encrypted backup to the user's own cloud drive** (their OneDrive or Google Drive, their storage), serving as disaster recovery, multi-device sync, and household-sharing transport. Encryption is protected by a master password or a locally-generated recovery key shown once at setup. The app never holds the key, so there is intentionally **no "forgot password" flow.**

**Multi-device sync model, as designed:** the encrypted cloud snapshot is the current state rather than just a backup, pulled and decrypted locally, worked against fully offline, and pushed back. Three layered mechanisms were designed against concurrent edits (an exclusive lock, a visible tap-required takeover, and a hard 30-minute lease), with last-write-wins plus a visible activity log underneath all three, deliberately not a CRDT. **What shipped instead, from 1.0.42.28, is the shared-folder snapshot sync described in the Technology stack section above, which merges three ways rather than locking.** The locking design is kept in full in `docs/CLAUDE-ARCHIVE-2026-09-14.md`, and is the step nobody has needed yet.

**Household sharing, two access tiers** reading from a shared cloud folder:

- *Free viewer companion.* Reads periodic snapshots from a natively-shared cloud folder, modeled on Anki shared decks (one-way periodic reimport, no live subscription). Sees meal schedule, shopping list and trend reports. One narrow write exception: **checking off shopping-list items** and marking scheduled tasks done, which are idempotent, single-field, low-conflict actions sent back as a small delta file, needing no merge logic.
- *Paid interactive companion.* Read-write access to a shared "household domain" (meal plan, shopping list, schedule) but **never** the primary's personal health domain (symptoms, labs, supplements, scoring history), which stays unreachable by any companion account.

Cross-provider sharing (OneDrive on one end, Google Drive on the other) does **not** require matching providers. The sync mechanism that makes that work is deliberately deferred to a design pass of its own.

**Sharing between people: the `.is` format.** `.is` is intended as the one general-purpose file format for anything shareable: recipes and favorites (built), plus future gardening, exercise and other non-health content. A person's health domain stays outside it, since that is the tier model's job. Build the payload shape generically from day one (an envelope type per content kind), not recipe-specific.

The standing security target, in the owner's words: "for data to be shared between two people who have the app, one of them had to send an invitation from the app to the other and the other had to accept it for their encrypted link to be associated to each other. If that encryption code is wrong from the person when you receive the data then it isn't accepted. Only the app knows what that code is between the two." Alongside that, a standing **Connections roster** so a repeat share doesn't need re-pairing, and OS-level `.is` file registration so tapping a received file from WhatsApp, email, or a file manager opens this app directly.

**Ordered prerequisite list for the security work** (given directly 2026-08-15; the full seven-step list with the reasoning behind each step's place in the order is in `docs/CLAUDE-ARCHIVE-2026-09-14.md`). Steps 1, 2, 3 and 5 are built, confirmed by reading the code on 2026-09-06: `tweetnacl` is a dependency, `lib/deviceIdentity.ts` keeps a private key in `expo-secure-store`, `connections` stores a unique public key per person, and `decodeShareEnvelope` in `lib/sharing.ts` verifies every received payload against the verbatim signed bytes, rejecting outright on mismatch. Step 4 became two-way in 1.0.34.30, and step 6, `.is` OS-level file-type registration, is in `app.json`'s intent filters. **Step 7, extending past recipes to gardening, exercise and the rest, is still open, alongside transport**, and should be designed and built together with tier-separation work rather than as a phase beforehand.

**Reminders and scheduling: contextual from day one, not clock-based.** "Take your levothyroxine" should account for what has actually been logged, since a calcium-rich food earlier pushes the safe window later. Two mechanisms: fully local on-device scheduled notifications for anything self-contained, and a minimal **content-blind** push relay (APNs/FCM) purely for cross-device wake-up signals, never carrying health content. Background execution is throttled on both mobile OSes, so instant recomputation is only guaranteed in the foreground; design around that. Reminders show their freshness ("based on food logged as of 7:45am"), because a confidently-wrong contextual reminder is worse than an honestly-uninformed static one.

**Interaction rules engine, two kinds, kept visually distinct:**

- *Built-in cited rules.* Prescription, supplement, and food timing interactions (levothyroxine with calcium or iron, biotin interfering with thyroid assays), authored with the same citation discipline as the nutrition database. Should ship as a versioned data file updatable independently of app releases.
- *Personal rule builder* (built 2026-08-18). The person's own discovered pattern or their doctor's specific instruction, always labeled distinctly.
- *Synergy to build toward.* The trend engine surfacing candidate personal rules from observed patterns: "you've logged fatigue within a day of high-goitrogen meals four times this month, want to turn that into a rule?" The Pattern Finder to rule shortcut is the natural next step and was explicitly deferred out of the 2026-08-18 build.

**Late and retroactive logging** must be low-friction: guided quick-select (what did you eat, pick from favorites or recents, when did it happen, smart time picker), never a blank form. Every late entry immediately shows its consequence (recalculated schedule impact), turning logging into a teaching moment. Scheduled reminders double as catch-up checkpoints.

**Reporting.** Doctor, nutritionist, trainer and self reports are different views over the same timestamped, taggable log data, with every entry timestamped and taggable from the start so reporting is a query rather than a rebuild. Reports generate entirely on-device (PDF export, no server rendering), consistent with the privacy model. The laid-out PDF export shipped 2026-09-14 (1.0.37.37): one `ReportDocument` rendered two ways, plain text on screen and HTML through `expo-print` for the PDF, shared through `expo-sharing`. Since 1.0.52.7 there are eight: the Overview plus a report per reader (doctor, nutritionist, trainer, caregiver) and per subject (looking back, medical costs, garden), each defined in `lib/reportKinds.ts` by which core sections it carries and which lens sections it adds, and `buildReport(days, kind)` never gathers a core section the report leaves out.

## Healing-journey stages

Decided 2026-07-31. Both halves (self-declaration with advisory, and reordering) shipped 2026-08-09 across 6 staged conditions: Hashimoto's, IBS, Celiac, IBD, Chronic Kidney Disease, and Gout. That is the full list of citable staged-food candidates across the 19.

Implementation: `lib/healingStage.ts`, `lib/healingStageAdvisory.ts`, `lib/conditionStages.ts`, `lib/conditionStageAdvisory.ts`, `lib/foodStageReordering.ts`, plus per-condition advisory modules, wired into all direct-ingredient Food builders, Profile, and `components/FoodLookup.tsx`.

**Framework:** the 5-stage healing and regression model (distinct from disease-progression staging), associated with Dr. Izabella Wentz: 1. Triage, 2. Digging, 3. Gut Repair, 4. Rebalancing, 5. Maintenance. Self-declared at setup, then adjusted over time by the existing Assessment. The app *suggests* a stage change and never silently reclassifies.

**What stage does: advisory and reordering only, never gating.** Stage-appropriate foods surface first, and anything questionable gets a color-coded flag linked to an explanation. Nothing is ever hidden or blocked.

**Evidence honesty.** Of the supplied sources, exactly one claim carries strong trial-level evidence: selenium reducing TPO antibodies, backed by a meta-analysis of 21 RCTs. Everything else is practitioner framework, case report or survey data. The thiamine claim is a **3-patient case report**, not the "landmark clinical pilot trial" it was described as; gluten, dairy and soy trigger rankings come from one practitioner's self-reported audience survey; and the staging framework is its author explaining it. That is fine to build on **provided the app labels it honestly.** The full source table with per-claim tiers is in the archive.

**Practical scoping:** only stages 2 and 3 meaningfully drive *food* decisions. Stages 1, 4, and 5 are about hormone dosing, nutrient correction, adaptogens, sleep, and labs. Five stages are right for education, but builder rules will key off fewer distinctions.

## The Unified Whole-Foods Database

`unified-database/` is a separate SQLite database (`unified_foods.sqlite`, ~115MB) and Node.js pipeline started 2026-08-10, built to become a richer, fully-auditable successor to `assets/data/foods_reference.db`. **Not merged, not wired to the app in any way.** `lib/db.ts` never reads from it and no screen touches it, so it is safe to iterate on indefinitely with zero risk to the live app.

**Why:** the current reference DB is the output of a one-time ad hoc Python filter script whose reasoning was never captured as testable rules. The unified pipeline rebuilds it reproducibly, discarding nothing (every original record survives in `raw_foods.raw_json`), classifying by a versioned unit-tested rule engine, matching across sources by species, Latin-name, and LanguaL evidence rather than fuzzy name guessing, and reviewing human-in-the-loop via a published audit tool.

**Architecture:** `sources` (9 registered) feeds `raw_foods`, which feeds `raw_food_nutrients` and `whole_food_classifications` (with a `reviewed` flag protecting human overrides from automated re-runs), which feed `food_match_groups` and `food_match_members`. One script per pipeline stage lives in `pipeline/`, each with a DB-backed test suite (391 passing). One adapter per source lives in `sources/`, so adding source #10 means registering it plus writing one adapter.

**Status (this drifts, so verify via `sqlite3.exe unified_foods.sqlite` rather than trusting these numbers):** all 9 sources ingested, classified and matched, roughly 32,700 raw records and 14,500 match groups, with about 16,700 classified whole-food, 13,100 not, and 2,950 unresolved. The audit tool is live at https://claude.ai/code/artifact/51c33d40-cbd9-4468-90b0-e1e460fd5b1d, and review has started (242 classification decisions applied) but is nowhere near complete.

**Phases:** 1 (schema and pipeline) and 2 (ingestion) are done. **3 and 4 (human review) have tooling done but review barely started, and this is the standing next step.** 5 (merge the app's curated D1-D6 and condition scores, aliases, and interaction rules onto the new master rather than re-deriving them), 6 (automated integrity verification), and 7 (the atomic reversible swap, gated on `REFERENCE_DB_VERSION`) are not started. Nothing here is urgent or blocking.

**If picking this up fresh:** read `unified-database/README.md` in full first.

## Named, unresolved risks (product, not engineering)

1. **Logging discipline and retention.** The single biggest risk to the whole vision is whether a person will log consistently enough, over weeks and months, for contextual reminders and trend correlation to mean anything. Most health-tracking apps fail here, not on missing features. Worth testing this assumption cheaply before building the full data model around it.
2. **Signal quality.** Whether symptom and food correlation-finding on noisy, confounded, n=1 personal data surfaces patterns versus things that merely look like patterns. Must be handled honestly in-product, showing confidence and never asserting causation.
